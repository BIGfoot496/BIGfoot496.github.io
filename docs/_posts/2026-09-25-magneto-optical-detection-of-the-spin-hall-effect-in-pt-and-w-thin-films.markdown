## A paper a day: day 15
Today's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06714v2) is a continuation of my exploration of ways of measuring spin Hall effect. This time, the technique uses magneto-optic Kerr effect: spin Hall effect leads to spin accumulation on the surface of the wire, and thus, if you light the wire up with a laser, the polarization of reflected light rotates. The spatial distribution of accumulated spins depends on sample thickness. So if you measure Kerr rotation for a bunch of samples of various thicknesses, you can extract the spin Hall angle. The coolest part of this technique is that it allows to measure the SHE more or less directly, unlike pretty much every single other method I've found so far, which all require the SHE material to interact somehow with a ferromagnet.
