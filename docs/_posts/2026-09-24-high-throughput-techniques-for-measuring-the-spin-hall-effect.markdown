## A paper a day: day 14
Today's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03543) is again for work, as will probably be several following ones. I'm trying to figure out methods for measuring SHE (awful abbreviation, makes it annoying to search), and this paper was a great start for that.

The three methods covered here are for sure not the only ones, but afaict from reading other papers all three are reasonably commonly used nowadays. They are: terahertz emission spectroscopy (TSE), vector-network analyzer ferromagnetic resonance (VNA FMR) and harmonic Hall response (HHR). TSE: smack sample with femtosecond laser, catch the induced terahertz emission, intensity gives spin Hall angle. VNA FMR: lay sample on waveguide with gigahertz current, measure waveguide's transmission as function of external magnetic field, do some maths jiggery-pokery and get spin Hall angle. HHR: lithography a cross in your sample, drive an AC current through one arm, measure voltage across the other as function of external magnetic field angle.

I'm kinda hoping I'll have a chance to try TSE with my own hands, 'cuz it sounds like pure sci-fi magic, but that one feels like the least generally accessible method. More likely I'll be doing more "conventional" measurements with Hall crosses and a bunch of lithography, ion beams, and pretty electron microscope photos.
