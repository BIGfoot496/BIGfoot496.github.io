## A paper a day: day 10
Today's [paper](https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=uc1.32106014644170&seq=198) is the first installment of linguistics here. I haven't read ling papers for months, turns out I missed them :-) This one is mostly a giant sequence of cognate lists together with a reconstruction of the POc root, but there were a few curious comments that make it worth reading. The funniest one is the coconut issue: a lot of Oceanic langs have a bunch (7-9) of distinct words designating various growth stages of a coconut (like, green and ready to drink vs dry and ready to fall, etc). That is not surprising by itself. However, the words different languages use for these different coconuts _are not cognates_. The semantic categories are very stable across the board, but it seems like everyone made their own terms for them.

Of course, I read the paper with Te Aka Māori dictionary trying to figure out which reflexes survived. One funny find was that _renga_ (yellow) comes from PEOc *reŋ(ʷ)a that meant "turmeric". It makes sense, but still funny. Another find that I cannot find evidence for, but strongly suspect nonetheless is that _uri_ (offspring, descendant, kin) comes from POc *(s,j)uli(q) -- plant sucker, cutting, shoot. While that cognate set does not have words meaning "relative" and all of them are bananas, taro, or their parts, the fact that _hua_ can also mean descendant makes the plant metaphor more plausible to my eyes. Also, _pū_ (bunch, tuft, bundle) that according to wiktionary comes from POc *puqu (origin, base of a tree) could be related to POc *puŋu -- meaning "bunch".
