## A paper a day: day 9
I'm not gonna write about seven papers today, for each day I missed, but I already wrote a post about [this one](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee4251) before my laptop went unresponsive, I like it and I'd like to keep a link to it.

Saturn's north pole hexagon has been known for a while, but nothing similar has ever been seen on the south pole. Well, now, there's a decagon. Seemingly it only appeared around four years ago. A giant rotating decagon. Ain't it the coolest? The paper's authors took all observations of Saturn they could find and figured out, what the conditions around it are (it sits on a jet, next to a strong anticyclone), how it moves (mostly at a constant angular velocity but with a slight periodic wobble). Then they threw the data into a bunch of shallow water simulations, attempting to reproduce the decagon. They did get some stable 10 sided waves, but not exactly with the same location and speed as the real thing. But still cool they got anything at all. Maybe at some point someone will bother with a multilayered model, and get out more accurate results.
