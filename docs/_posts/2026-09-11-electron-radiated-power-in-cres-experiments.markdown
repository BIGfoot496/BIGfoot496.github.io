## A paper a day: day 6
Truth be told, today's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02844) was pretty dang hard for me to read. If you've read anything complicated ever, you know how it is: you see some topic mentioned that you are unfamiliar with, so you go search for it, and its explanation references something else you don't know, so you go research *that*, etc. So I ended up going through the derivation of Liénard–Wiechert fields, then the Larmor formula, and only then went back to the paper. That helped. A little bit.

The paper itself is about figuring out initial kinetic energy distribution of electrons by looking at the radiation they spew out while trapped in a waveguide in a "magnetic bottle". The coolest part is for sure that the magnetobremsstrahlung's spectrum in this case looks like a comb of equidistant delta functions, separated by axial frequency. That is the result of a LOT of Fourier transform magic that I could barely follow (because there's like three different periodic motions happening at the same time). 

All this electron whirling and twirling I read, while listening to Debussy's String Quartet in G minor and the album called "Sounds of atoms" by Sayer (found thanks to the track called Cyclotron, for obvious reasons). Y'know what, I say scientists should compile soundtracks for their papers, just like TTRPG players do for their characters. Would science be better off for it? No idea, prolly not. But it sure as heck would be fun!
