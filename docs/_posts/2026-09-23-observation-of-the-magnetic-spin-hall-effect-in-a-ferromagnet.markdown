## A paper a day: day 13
Today's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25406v1) is for work, rather than purely for fun. I still don't feel I understand everything the authors did, and why they did that, but here's what I got. They made a device for producing and measuring spin Hall effect in a choice material. The material in question -- a stack of thin films of Co and Pt, which really wants to be magnetised perpendicularly to the stack. (Note to self: read up on the methods of fabrication of such devices.) Then, if an electric current $$I_{\text{bias}}$$ is passed along the stack (dubbed in this case the _injector_), for reasons that are still unclear to me, electrons with opposite directions of spin get deflected in the opposite directions, perpendicular to the current and the magnetisation of the injector. They flow through a magnetically neutral wire (copper in this case) and get measured at a magnetised _detector_ (here made out of permalloy). Apparently, (once again, for reasons unclear to me as of yet), the magnetisation of the injector forces a very different behaviour on spins with polarisation transverse to the bias current (conventional SHE, even under time reversal) and parallel to it (magnetic SHE, odd under time reversal). And if you exchange the injector and detector, you can use a spin current to induce an electric current. The "nonlocal resistance" (measured voltage on the detector over the bias current) is the same for the inverse effect, though. Yada, yada, they do a bunch of measurements on their device and confirm the above, as is expected from theory. 

Tbh, reading this was unnecessarily annoying for two reasons. Firstly, it felt like reading a novel from page 300: there's very clearly a lot of stuff the reader is expected to be familiar with, like, say, the conventional spin Hall effect, or the "lateral spin valve" architecture of their device. I am *un*familiar with a lot of that stuff, so it wasn't a very good paper to get into the topic. Secondly (maybe somewhat related to the first point), abbreviations are EVIL. When a single figure has six of them, I'd say this is too many abbreviations. Way too many.
