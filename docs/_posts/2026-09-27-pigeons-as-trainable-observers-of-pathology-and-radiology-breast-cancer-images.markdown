## A paper a day: day 17
Today's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0141357) is one of those "could be cool" kinds of experiment. Skinner did get an Ig Nobel for pigeon missiles, after all, so why can't pigeon histopathologists work just as well? And yup, they do. The experiment design was eerily similar to basic machine learning, complete with dataset augmentation via rotating and flipping the images. Looking at those tissue images myself, I really cannot tell which ones are benign, and which are malignant, but evidently, pigeons, who spent several weeks practicing every day, could do it quite easily. Mammograms proved much harder to distinguish, but human _experts_ kinda struggle with them too. Overall, an impressive performance from the avian "doctors"
