## A paper a day: day 1
Today's [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-3276(08)60373-1) is about the issues that arose when the old quantum theory met with the three body problem that is a helium atom, and the ways it was ultimately solved. This is not one of the original 1928-1929 Hylleraas papers -- those ones are in German, and I didn't want to bother. No, this is better: it's a paper (a book chapter, really), written by Hylleraas himself almost thirty years after the fact, overviewing the problem and the advances in the field that happened since then. The chapter uses techniques very familiar to someone who has studied QM in a modern university, to me it was kinda surprising, and very welcome. It was also cool to witness how the Hylleraas coordinates (sum of electrons' distances to the nucleus, their difference, and the distance between the electrons themselves) are so simple, yet so effective. With the amount of different *stuff* they tried, I'm really surprised this is what worked. I also chuckled every time there were references like "this has been used with considerable success by Hylleraas (1932)" (and boy, were there a lot of them). Scientists citing themselves in the third person will never stop being funny...
