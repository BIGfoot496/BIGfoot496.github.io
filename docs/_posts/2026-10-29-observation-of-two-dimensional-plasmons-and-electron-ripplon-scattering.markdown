## A paper a day: day 26
Could not find a good new paper to cover today, so decided to write about one that was mentioned at the conference several weeks ago and I promptly read during a particularly boring talk. This [paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.36.145) has basically created plasmonics as a field (not that I know anything at all about it, otherwise those lectures wouldn't have been so boring for me). To me, honestly, the most surprising thing in this paper was that it was written in 1975. To observe what they observed you need: to make a cell full of liquid helium, deposit electrons on its surface so that they get trapped in the image potential, induce density waves in the 2DEG you just made, and finally measure the rf absorption of this eldritch horror. At different frequencies. At different temperatures. I think it's bloomin' impressive what humanity could do fifty years ago! They even calculated the electron mobilities from the linewidths of the standing wave resonances and figured at which point they stop being scattered by helium vapour atoms and instead start scattering on surface tension waves (ripplons, really? physicists need to stop inventing quasiparticles and giving them names...)
