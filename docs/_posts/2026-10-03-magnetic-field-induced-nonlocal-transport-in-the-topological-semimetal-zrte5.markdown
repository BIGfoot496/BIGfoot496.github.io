## A paper a day: day 24
Today's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09720v2) was very interesting to me, because it is the closest thing I could find to someone already having performed the experiments I am (likely) going to do during my PhD programme. The material's not the same (at least I don't think so, I don't actually know yet), but it is a topological semimetal, so that's probably the best I'm gonna find in the literature.

In short, the authors took some pieces of ZrTe<sub>5</sub> crystal and placed up to 8 electrodes on them parallel to each other. They passed current between electrodes 1 and 2, and then detected nonzero voltages between electrode 8 and all the others, exponentially decaying with distance from the current drain (electrode 2). The nonlocal voltage was detected only when there was an external magnetic field parallel to the _a_ axis of the crystal, if the field direction was more than 1° off, the effect vanished. They also did some more standard Hall bar measurements, and also got a nonzero voltage across the bar away from the charge current. According to their simulations, the trivial explanations -- inhomogeneous current distribution and hence just Ohm's law, and Joule heating of the bar creating temperature gradients -- produce a decay length that is way smaller than the one they observed. Spin diffusion length in this material is known to be much larger than the ones they measured. So they conjectured the observed effects were a result of the current in zirconium pentatelluride polarizing some other degree of freedom (not spin) and this polarization travelling along the sample, in an effect similar to SHE. I'm not sure what to think about the results, really, I'm out of my depth here. But the experiment design feels to me like something worth stealing.
