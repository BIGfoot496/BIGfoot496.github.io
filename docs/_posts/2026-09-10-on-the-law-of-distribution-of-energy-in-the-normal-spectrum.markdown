## A paper a day: day 5
Today I spent reviewing blackbody radiation laws, hence this [paper](https://jgluza.us.edu.pl/QM/planck1901.pdf). I first read Planck's 1900 [paper](https://web.archive.org/web/20161010010043/http://www.ffn.ub.es/luisnavarro/nuevo_maletin/Planck%20%281900%29,%20Improvement%20of%20Wien%27s.pdf) (I strongly suspect it is rather a lecture transcript), and it's literally just "I love Wien's law, I find it beautiful, but it's sadly kinda wrong for large wavelengths. So I tinkered with a ton of expressions similar to Wien's law, and look at the cool formula I found as a result. It works really well, idk why" (not a quote). In contrast, the 1901 paper is basically the same as what we do in intro to stat mech while deriving Bose-Einstein distribution, just without the chemical potential. And also with Wien's *displacement* law postulated (not the same as Wien's law (which is really an approximation), because that's not confusing at all), together with a discretisation of vibrational energy. A bit annoying to read due to the aforementioned displacement law being presented as a relationship between energy density and wavelenght, which I was unfamiliar with, but ultimately not particularly different from how we did it in uni.
