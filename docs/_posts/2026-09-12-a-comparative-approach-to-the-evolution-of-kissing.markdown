## A paper a day: day 6
The 2026 Igs were held a couple weeks ago, I wanted a fun lighthearted read today, so I decided to read [one of the winning papers](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2025.106788). The prize was awarded for devising a more precise definition of kissing -- "non-agonistic interactions involving directed, intraspecific, oral-oral contact with some movement of the lips/mouthparts and no food transfer" -- in ants, birds, polar bears, and humans. The definition is indeed the most interesting part of the paper, as most of the rest was a plea for more data. They also did some complicated Monte Carlo Markov Chain modelling to predict whether kissing is an ancestral trait for Afro-Eurasian monkeys and apes, but I'm not sure the results are super trustworthy considering the sparcity of data. Although, I don't know anything about how these evolution models work, should probably read up on them, they sound like they have interesting maths.
